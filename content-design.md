# CAPIO Content Design System

Version: 1.0 — working specification, pending founder visual acceptance.

## Sources of truth
- CAPIO website: https://www.capioteknologi.co.id/

- Actual original logo is mandatory. Never approximate, redraw, or fabricate it.
- The founder-approved look takes precedence over generic generated moodboards.

## Observed website styling (2026-11-02)
- Typeface: Geist for headings and body (observed from live site).
- Main ink/deep navy: #080E22; secondary navy: #111B2E.
- Background: #0A1428; supporting copy: #AAB3C2.
- Accent blue: #2F6FED. Brand red only where consistent with approved logo and visuals.
- Tone: restrained, credible, modern, enterprise; not playful, generic cyberpunk, or fluorescent.

Exact social font sizes and spacing are NOT established solely by the website. Determine these by inspecting the provided original approved slides and founder review. Avoid blindly copying website pixel values to 1080x1350 artwork.

## Target
Decision makers in Indonesian banks, payment providers, fintech, insurers and large enterprises. Editorial sophistication and evidence matter more than visual effects.

## Visual concept families
1. Executive Advisory — audit, ISO, IT governance: refined editorial architecture / business / evidence photography, light neutral canvas, meaningful graphic information architecture.
2. Technical Intelligence — pentest, VAPT, API security: true infrastructure/editorial technical images, clear diagrams and restrained dark navy.
3. Institutional Evidence — registrations, certifications, client proof: official verifiable documentary references, accurate date/number, minimal decorative imagery. Never fabricate certificates or official logos.

These are not rigid templates or mere color switches. Each post must have its own art direction, composition, narrative rhythm and image crop. The brand system, not the layout, makes the posts recognizably CAPIO.

## Non-negotiable design rules
- Professional, clean, futuristic in a restrained enterprise sense; more useful pictures and fewer repeated icons.
- No hooded hackers, glowing padlocks, 3D seals, identical card rows, meaningless diagonal stripes, repetitive checkboxes, plastic AI mockups or excessive gradients.
- Distinct cover composition and visual story per topic.
- Carefully set type: comfortable body text, controlled headline size, balanced line breaks, spacing, and contrast at phone size.
- Use genuine CAPIO-owned or properly licensed media; original logo pixel-/vector-faithful.
- No invented facts, registrations, certificates, client work, regulation provisions or outcomes.
- Include persuasive CTA based on audience and claim, not boilerplate hard selling.
- 1080×1350 PNG per slide. Slide count based on story (1–4 normal; more only if genuinely justified).
- All final designs should be reproducible/editable with source HTML/CSS or other editable assets; composite moodboards are not deliverables.

## Production and approval sequence
1. Study the source artwork in full size and write a post-specific creative brief: buyer, single takeaway, evidence, storyline, tone, image treatment and CTA.
2. Produce TWO truly distinct cover concepts for each topic, not a palette swap; present at actual 1080×1350 for founder visual approval.
3. Only after selecting a cover direction, extend to the remaining slides.
4. Check correct brand asset, actual technical content, claims, safe margins, no clipping, hierarchy, 4:5 output, and consistency at mobile size.
5. Deliver individual slide PNGs + preview contact sheet + two platform-tailored captions + source notes + editable source manifest.
6. Preserve existing artwork. Upload revised files as versioned drafts, and verify folder contents before reporting upload success.
7. Technical reviewer and founder publication approval are separate. Any revision invalidates approval for the previous content version.

## Acceptance checklist
Reject if the design could belong to any random cybersecurity vendor; if all content topics share the same grid; if presentation is mostly repeated boxes or icons; if type hierarchy is too loud or too small; if images are generic or fabricated; if sources cannot substantiate claims; or if the result cannot be modified and rerendered by the content engine.
